## Decision
- Every TAPP product (CSpec::Separate) with GEMM-fusable A/B/D now plans on faer when K >= 512 or the output has <= 2^20 elements (plan-time rule; beta is an execution argument and cannot be used).
- Separate C with a large output and K < 512 stays on the packed driver: the D := beta*op_C(C) pass costs 20-50% of faer's time on memory-bound shapes (e.g. gemm_batched_003: 0.63 -> 0.48, 013: 1.50 -> 0.88, 012: 2.71 -> 1.28 at 8T), so faer loses there at 4T/8T.
- beta == 0 needs no pass: faer matches the overwrite result (workload 1.078 / 1.173 / 1.249 at 1T/4T/8T); the small-case gains (geomean 1.5x, tiny GEMMs 2-5x) are kept.
- The faer in-place scale was serial; the parallel output pass replaces it (separate_same at 8T up to 1.2x faster on big cases, neutral at 1T).
- Kept on packed: separate C with out > 2^20 and K < 512 (plan-time, whatever beta); an isolated one-input K axis (unchanged); non-fusable groups (unchanged).
- Caveat: the corpus has one c64 case; the thresholds were fit on f64. Pre-existing: faer already loses to packed on some memory-bound overwrite shapes (S0 gate FAIL); not changed here.
- The final rule was evaluated on the measured data, not re-measured as a whole (a routed-to-packed case is the packed row by construction).
- Note: with beta known to be 0 at plan time faer would win everywhere (workload 1.249 at 8T vs 1.093 under the rule); beta is an execution argument, so the rule assumes it may be nonzero. A lazily built second plan is a possible follow-up.
